About

The course of a legal matter is often decided before any lawsuit is filed. The office has been practising in Şehitkamil, Gaziantep since early 2025, providing legal assistance to individuals and companies.

Every matter is handled in the same way. First, the facts and available documents are reviewed and missing evidence is identified. Running and upcoming deadlines are listed at the outset. Then the route is chosen: mediation, a formal notice, enforcement proceedings or a lawsuit. Clients are told not only which route was chosen, but also why the others were not.

Cases are followed electronically before courts and enforcement offices across Türkiye through the national judiciary network (UYAP). Hearings, attachments and site inspections are attended by the office. Advisory work for companies also covers the stage before a dispute arises.

Working principles

  • Clear communication. Each stage, its possible outcomes and its deadlines are explained in plain language. Where there is uncertainty, it is stated as such.
  • Deadline discipline. Service, hearing and appeal deadlines are tracked; many deadlines in Turkish law are strict and cannot be restored once missed.
  • Evidence first. Claims and defences are built on documents, and the source of each piece of evidence is planned early.
  • Realistic assessment. The weaknesses of a claim are shared as openly as its strengths.
  • Confidentiality. All information shared within the attorney-client relationship is protected as professional secrecy under Turkish law.

Working with clients abroad

Power of attorney. Clients outside Türkiye can grant a power of attorney at a Turkish embassy or consulate, or before a local notary. A power of attorney issued by a foreign notary must bear an apostille (or consular legalisation for countries outside the Hague Convention) and be translated into Turkish by a sworn translator and certified by a Turkish notary before it can be used.

Following the case. Proceedings can be followed electronically throughout Türkiye, and in most matters the client does not need to travel. Hearing dates, filings and decisions are reported to the client in Turkish or English.

Documents. Foreign-language documents to be submitted to Turkish courts generally need a sworn Turkish translation; some official documents also require an apostille.

Fees. Fees are agreed in writing in an attorney engagement agreement. Under Turkish law, they cannot be lower than the minimum fee schedule published annually by the Union of Turkish Bar Associations. Court costs, translation and notary expenses are separate from the attorney fee.

Frequently asked questions

How can I schedule a meeting?

Send an e-mail to info@avmugurkilic.com with a short description of your matter. Meetings are held at the office by appointment. If a deadline is running, for example after receiving a court or enforcement notice, please mention it so the request can be prioritised.

Is what I share kept confidential?

Yes. The attorney's duty of professional secrecy arises from the law and continues even if the matter is not taken on. Please do not send sensitive personal data or case documents through the website form.

Are initial consultations free of charge?

No. Legal consultations are charged and are subject to the minimum fee schedule of the Union of Turkish Bar Associations. The current schedule is published by the Union of Turkish Bar Associations.

Do I have to go to mediation before filing a lawsuit?

In several types of disputes, yes. Mediation is a mandatory precondition for monetary commercial claims, most employment claims, and disputes arising from leases, co-ownership and neighbour relations. A lawsuit filed without first applying to mediation is dismissed on procedural grounds.

How long does a case take?

No fixed duration can be given. It depends on the court's workload, the number of parties, whether expert examination is needed and whether the decision is appealed. A realistic estimate is shared during the meeting, based on similar matters. This is an estimate, not a commitment.

Can you guarantee the outcome?

No. No attorney can guarantee the outcome of litigation. What can be done is to set out clearly the strengths and weaknesses of the claim, the likely defences and the possible results.
